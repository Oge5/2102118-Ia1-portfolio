My Traits & Hobbies

Three qualities that shape who I am and how I approach my studies.

1. Adaptability

One of my strongest personality traits is adaptability. I consider myself adaptable because I am able to adjust when there are new situations, new tasks, or new challenges ahead of me. As a student that deal with different assignments, group activities, and sometimes new software, being adaptable helps me to not give up when something does not go the way I expected it.

2. Resilience

Another personality trait is being resilien. I believe i am resilient because when I get a bad grade I try to not let that make me give up. If I fail a test or get a bad grade on my assignments I try to learn from my mistakes and use the experience to push myself to do better. In my academic life resilience helps me stay motivated and continue working toward my goals even when I face challenges.

3. Watching Movies

Watching movies is my top favourite hobby. I love to watch movies because it always entertained me and give me a chance to take my mind off school and work. When I feel stressed, bored, or feel like I need a break i watch a movie to relax for a while. This hobby has a positive impact on my academic life because I believe taking breaks is important when dealing with a busy school schedule.

My Hero

About Natalie Bennett

Natalie Bennett is the main character in the 2022 movie Look Both Ways. She is a college student who is preparing to start the next stage of her life when she discovers that she is pregnant. The movie follows two different possibilities for her life, showing how one unexpected situation can change someone's plans.

My Favourite Scene

My favourite scene is the bathroom pregnancy test scene because it shows how one moment near the end of college can completely change the direction of someone's future. I also like how the movie uses the split-screen idea to show two different versions of Natalie's life. It shows that even when life takes an unexpected path, that does not mean that one version of your life is a failure.

Why She Inspires Me

This scene is meaningful to me because I became pregnant in my final year and at first I felt like my life and career has set back. But watching Look Both Ways helped me realise that I am not the only person who has experienced a major life change while trying to complete school and build a career. Natalie inspires me because she shows that an unexpected change can become the beginning of a new chapter rather than the end of your plans.

LLMs: Tool or Crutch?

A reflection on the role of Large Language Models in my learning as a student.

When I read the statement "LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot must be used as a tool not a crutch" I understand the meaning it is trying to get across. An LLM can assist me in thinking and learning but it should not replace the parts of studying that help build my skills. The word "crutch" here stands for something that supports you so you don't have to do the work yourself. If every time I do an assignment or task that requires my personal understanding and I rely on an LLM for my final answer then i dont really learn anything at all and I take away the opportunity of doing my own thinking. I no longer practice the skills education is supposed to build. The guidance by Stanford for students reflects that using generative AI to do the major part of your work takes learning away from you (AI and Your Learning: A Guide for Students, 2021) .

Using an LLM as a tool on the other hand is different. It means I still do the main work while the model acts as a helpful assistant. I can use it to explain a hard concept in simpler words, suggest an outline, or help me spot mistakes in my own writing. When I finish a draft of an essay I can ask it to point out unclear parts, and then I fix them myself. For my understanding I can ask for step-by-step explanations and have multiple perspectives on it. When I am studying for a test it can create practice questions by turning my own notes and lecture powerpoint into practice questions and then I test myself by writing my own answers and ask it to cross check. So by doing this I used LLM as a learning partner instead of an answer source.

An LLM is most likely to become a crutch when I no longer do the work. This happens when I ask for the final answer instead of feedback, submitting an AI output without understanding it, fail to check references, or avoid studying because the notes were already generated for me. In order to prevent this my rule is simply to treat anything that an AI produce as a starting point and not a finished product. Using course materials to verify accuracy, and write the final reasoning in my own words. If I cannot explain an answer in my own words, I know I have not actually understood it.

For me "LLM as a tool, not a crutch" is a way of using AI to speed up the parts of the learning process but at the same time maintaining my own understanding. I think the point of education is not just to answer questions but to develop the skill of answering and defending them. When I use LLMs to support that process they become a tool but if they are used as shortcuts to avoid thinking then they become a crutch.
